INTRO
Hot tubs and pools are among the most code-sensitive things you can add to a house, and the rules exist for good reason. This is the one backyard project where a shortcut can be fatal. TLB Electric wires hot tubs and pools throughout Dieppe with proper GFCI protection, a disconnect at the required distance, and correct bonding of everything metallic around the water.

WHAT TO EXPECT
A dedicated 240V circuit sized to the equipment spec, with a GFCI-protected disconnect at the code-required distance from the water and bonding of the shell, rails, and surrounding metal. Includes conduit run and buried to code, a panel capacity check, and TISNB inspection.

BODY COPY
The hookup itself is a small job. What drives the price in Dieppe is distance and route.

A lot of Dieppe homes sit on generous lots with the deck well back from the house, and the panel is typically in a basement at the opposite corner. That can mean a substantial run, and whether it goes through finished basement ceiling, gets trenched underground, or takes an exterior route changes both the cost and how invasive the work is. We walk the route with you before quoting, because a number given without seeing where the tub is going is a guess.

The code requirements are not negotiable and we don't treat them as optional. A hot tub needs GFCI protection, and it needs a disconnect that's reachable but at the required distance from the water, far enough that nobody can touch it from inside the tub. Everything metallic in the vicinity has to be bonded together, which is the requirement most often skipped by unlicensed installs and the one that matters most. Bonding is what prevents a difference in potential between, say, a metal railing and the water. This is the part of the job where doing it properly is the entire point.

We see the consequences of shortcuts often enough. The common ones are a tub fed from an existing outdoor receptacle rather than a dedicated circuit, a disconnect mounted at arm's reach from the water, and bonding omitted entirely. All three fail inspection, and all three are genuinely dangerous.

Panel capacity is the other thing to check first. A hot tub is a substantial continuous load, and on a Dieppe home already carrying a heat pump and an EV charger it may be the addition that requires a service upgrade. Better to know that before the tub is delivered and sitting on the driveway.

For pools, the bonding requirements extend further, to the pool structure, any metal within a defined distance, and the pump and heater equipment. Above-ground and in-ground have different requirements. Tell us which you're installing and we'll scope it accordingly.
