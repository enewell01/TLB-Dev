Winter storms and high wind cause a fair share of Dieppe's electrical emergencies each year: downed lines, damaged weatherheads, and services knocked loose enough to arc. Not every outage is a utility problem, and part of an emergency call is figuring out quickly whether it's the utility's issue or something on your side of the meter that needs immediate repair.

Dieppe's mix of residential subdivisions and the commercial strip around Champlain Place means emergency calls range from a homeowner with a tripped main breaker that won't reset to a retail unit that's lost power to half its lighting circuits during business hours. Both get treated with the same urgency, and both get a clear price before any work starts, even outside regular hours.

If your situation can safely wait until morning, such as a single dead outlet or a breaker that resets fine on its own, we'll tell you that honestly rather than upsell an emergency call you don't need. But for anything involving heat, sparking, smoke, or a total loss of power with no reported utility outage, we respond as an emergency.
